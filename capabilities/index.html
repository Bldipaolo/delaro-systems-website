Possible interventions

Different problems require different forms of change.

Capabilities are building materials, not product packages. A credible architecture may use one, several, or none of the technologies below.

01

Process redesign

Clarify ownership, eliminate unnecessary work, simplify approvals, improve handoffs, and redesign the operating flow before adding software.

  • Future-state workflows
  • Roles and decision rights
  • Exception and approval design
02

Integrations

Move reliable information between the systems that already support the business, reducing breaks between tools and teams.

  • API connections
  • Data synchronization
  • System-of-record alignment
03

Automation

Automate repetitive, stable, low-ambiguity work where the operating case is measurable and failure can be handled safely.

  • Rules and routing
  • Document and data movement
  • Notifications and scheduling
04

AI & decision support

Use probabilistic systems for interpretation, extraction, synthesis, or recommendations while preserving appropriate human responsibility.

  • Classification and extraction
  • Research and synthesis
  • Human-in-the-loop decisions
05

Internal applications

Build focused interfaces around the way teams actually work when generic software creates more friction than it removes.

  • Operational workspaces
  • Approval interfaces
  • Role-specific tools
06

Operational intelligence

Create the data structures, dashboards, reporting flows, and signals needed to understand performance and make timely decisions.

  • Dashboards and reports
  • Data infrastructure
  • Operational alerts

Selection logic

Choose by certainty, judgment, and consequence.

Known, repeatable rules usually belong in deterministic software. Ambiguous information may benefit from AI. High-consequence actions usually require human approval.

The architecture can combine these modes without asking one technology to do work it is poorly suited to perform.

Known rulesDeterministic execution

Predictable inputs, stable logic, reversible actions.

Variable contextAI-assisted work

Interpretation, synthesis, generation, recommendation.

Material consequenceHuman decision

Approval, accountability, exceptions, and judgment.

Architecture principles

Operational reliability is part of the solution.

A technically impressive system that cannot be understood, recovered, audited, or measured is not operationally complete.

Reliability
Design predictable behavior, explicit failure modes, and recovery paths.
Security
Limit access, protect credentials and data, and preserve appropriate controls.
Auditability
Make important decisions, approvals, and system actions traceable.
Maintainability
Favor clear components and client-owned accounts over unnecessary lock-in.
Human intervention
Keep people in the loop where risk, ambiguity, or responsibility requires them.
Measurement
Instrument the change around business outcomes, not activity counts.

What this means

The same request can lead to a different answer.

A request for an AI follow-up agent might reveal a routing problem, missing ownership, poor data, inconsistent messaging, or a capacity constraint. The right answer could be AI—or a rule, an integration, a process change, or no automation at all.
