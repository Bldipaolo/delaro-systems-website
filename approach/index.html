Diagnosis before design.

A system is only valuable when it addresses a problem worth solving. Delaro begins with the business, maps the operation, establishes the economics, and earns the right to recommend an intervention.

Business contextMeasured outcome

Begin with context

See the company before isolating the workflow.

An operational problem rarely belongs to one task. It sits inside a business model, a set of responsibilities, a system landscape, and a chain of downstream consequences.

We learn what the company sells, how value is created, who owns the work, which systems hold the truth, where decisions happen, and what depends on the outcome. That context prevents a local fix from creating a larger problem somewhere else.

Diagnostic sequence

Reduce uncertainty before investment increases.

Each stage produces evidence for the next. A recommendation can move backward when the facts change.

  1. 01

    Understand

    Learn the business model, roles, systems, economics, and constraints.

  2. 02

    Map

    Document triggers, inputs, work, decisions, outputs, and dependencies.

  3. 03

    Diagnose

    Separate visible symptoms from the constraint or root cause producing them.

  4. 04

    Quantify

    Establish frequency, labor, delay, error, risk, and addressable value.

  5. 05

    Prioritize

    Compare opportunities by impact, difficulty, risk, measurability, and leverage.

  6. 06

    Architect

    Design the future-state process and simplest reliable supporting system.

  7. 07

    Implement

    Build, integrate, test, document, and introduce the change responsibly.

  8. 08

    Measure

    Compare real performance with the baseline and agreed success criteria.

  9. 09

    Improve

    Refine the system and use new evidence to identify the next constraint.

Root cause

Diagnose the mechanism, not the symptom.

“The team needs automation” is not a diagnosis. The cause may be ownership, routing, data quality, approval design, system interoperability, or work that should not exist.

Delaro traces the issue across people, process, information, and technology until the underlying constraint can be stated clearly enough to test.

Observed symptomDelay, rework, missed information
Operating mechanismOwnership, handoff, rule, system, or data
Business consequenceCost, capacity, revenue, risk, or decision quality

Economic diagnosis

Determine what is worth fixing.

The objective is not a dramatic theoretical maximum. It is a credible estimate of realistically recoverable value.

Establish the baseline

Measure current volume, time, labor cost, delay, errors, missed opportunities, and downstream effects.

Separate the evidence

Distinguish known values, client assumptions, Delaro assumptions, estimates, and post-deployment measurements.

Model addressable value

Evaluate labor savings, capacity, revenue recovery, error reduction, avoided hiring, working capital, and risk where evidence permits.

Solution architecture

Use the simplest reliable intervention.

Architecture follows the diagnosed problem. Deterministic rules handle known logic. AI is reserved for interpretation, synthesis, generation, and flexible decision support.

Where risk is meaningful, the system preserves approval, auditability, recovery, and human intervention. Complexity is accepted only when it produces a stronger operational result.
